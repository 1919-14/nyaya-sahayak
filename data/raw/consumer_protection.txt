DOMAIN: Consumer Protection
SOURCE: Consumer Protection Act, 2019

Section 2(7) — Definition of consumer.
A consumer means any person who buys goods or hires/avails services for consideration, and
includes any user of such goods or beneficiary of such services, but does not include a person
who obtains goods for resale or commercial purpose.

Section 2(11) — Deficiency.
Deficiency means any fault, imperfection, shortcoming, or inadequacy in the quality, nature,
and manner of performance which is required to be maintained by or under any law, or has been
undertaken to be performed by a person in pursuance of a contract.

Section 34 — Jurisdiction of District Commission.
A complaint may be filed with the District Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission where the
value of goods or services paid does not exceed one crore rupees. Complaints may be filed
where the opposite party resides or carries on business, or where the cause of action arose,
or where the complainant resides or works.

Section 35 — Manner of filing complaint.
A complaint may be filed by the consumer, a recognized consumer association, the Central or
State Government, or one or more consumers on behalf of numerous consumers with the same
interest. Complaints can be filed in person, by an authorized agent, by post, or electronically.

Section 38 — Procedure on admission of complaint.
On admission, the Commission shall refer a copy of the complaint to the opposite party and
direct them to give a response within thirty days, extendable by fifteen days.

Section 39 — Reliefs available.
Reliefs that can be ordered include: removal of defect, replacement of goods, return of price
paid, payment of compensation for loss or injury suffered, discontinuation of unfair trade
practice, and payment of adequate costs.

Typical grounds for complaint.
Common consumer disputes include: defective products, deficient services (e.g. banking,
telecom, insurance, e-commerce delivery), unfair trade practices, misleading advertisements,
and overcharging above MRP.

Practical note on filing.
No court fee is required for claims up to five lakh rupees. A consumer should retain purchase
receipts, warranty cards, and written communication with the seller/service provider as
evidence before filing.

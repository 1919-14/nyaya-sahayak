DOMAIN: Workplace and Labour Rights
SOURCE: Payment of Wages Act 1936; Industrial Disputes Act 1947; Code on Wages 2019 (illustrative summary)

Timely payment of wages.
Wages must be paid before the expiry of the 7th day (establishments with fewer than 1000
workers) or the 10th day (establishments with 1000 or more workers) of the following wage
period. Unauthorized deductions from wages are restricted to specific categories such as fines,
absence from duty, damage to goods entrusted, and statutory deductions.

Termination and notice.
A permanent workman who has been in continuous service for at least one year generally cannot
be retrenched without one month's notice (or wages in lieu) and retrenchment compensation at
15 days' average pay for every completed year of continuous service, along with notice to the
appropriate government where applicable.

Wrongful termination remedy.
A worker who believes their termination was illegal or without due process may raise an
industrial dispute. This typically starts with a conciliation attempt through the labour
department before proceeding to an Industrial Tribunal or Labour Court.

Sexual harassment at workplace.
Under the Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act,
2013, every employer with 10 or more employees must constitute an Internal Complaints Committee.
A complaint must generally be filed within three months of the incident, extendable by the
Committee for sufficient reason.

Minimum wages.
Every employer is required to pay at least the minimum wage notified for the relevant
scheduled employment and state/region. Non-payment of minimum wages can be reported to the
labour inspector or Assistant Labour Commissioner of the area.

Practical note on filing a labour complaint.
A worker can typically file a written complaint with the local Labour Commissioner's office.
Useful evidence includes appointment letter, salary slips, attendance records, and any written
communication regarding the dispute.
